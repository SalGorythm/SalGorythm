Claude will not magically land you a dream job this month. What it will do—if you treat it like a structured coach instead of a hype machine—is compress the boring parts of a job search: positioning, resume iteration, outreach drafts, mock interviews, and weekly planning. After nine years hiring and being hired across IBM, EY, and senior data-science loops, I use Claude the same way I use it in production: clear roles, explicit constraints, and outputs I verify before they touch a recruiter.

This article is an operating system of eight prompts you can run in sequence or pick à la carte. Paste your real résumé, target role, and constraints. Ask Claude to stay truthful—no invented metrics, no fake certifications.

## How to run these prompts well

Before prompt one, give Claude a **context block** you reuse across sessions:

- Current résumé (text, not PDF screenshots)
- Target role title and level (e.g., senior data scientist, staff ML engineer)
- 5–10 target companies or industries
- Non-negotiables: location, remote, compensation floor, visa, start date
- Your top three measurable wins from the last two roles

Tell Claude: *"Flag assumptions. Do not invent employers, dates, or metrics. Ask clarifying questions before rewriting my résumé."*

That single constraint separates useful output from LinkedIn-bro fiction.

## 1. Dream Job Operating System

Use this first when you need a master plan—not another generic cover letter.

```text
Act as a world-class executive recruiter, hiring manager, career strategist,
LinkedIn consultant, interview coach, and organizational psychologist. Build a
complete operating system for helping me secure my dream position.

Analyze my experience, skills, achievements, goals, target companies, desired
role, and competitive advantages. Design my job search strategy, resume
optimization, interview preparation, salary negotiation, and long-term career
roadmap.
```

**What to expect:** A phased plan—target list, narrative angle, weekly rhythm, and negotiation framing. Push back if it suggests applying everywhere; narrow beats spray-and-pray.

## 2. Career Positioning

Run this when you are unsure which lane fits your background—IC vs manager, platform vs product ML, consulting vs in-house.

```text
Act as a senior recruiter specializing in my target industry. Analyze my
background and identify the roles, companies, and opportunities where I have
the highest probability of success.

Compare my profile with current hiring expectations, identify skill gaps,
highlight competitive strengths, and create a positioning strategy that makes
me a compelling candidate. Recommend projects, certifications, or portfolio
improvements that would strengthen my profile.
```

**Senior move:** Ask for a table—*role fit score*, *gap*, *proof you already have*, *30-day fix*. Skip vanity certs unless a specific job family requires them.

## 3. Resume and portfolio optimization

Claude is strong at tightening bullets if you feed it numbers it can verify.

```text
Act as a hiring manager and ATS optimization expert. Transform my resume and
portfolio into assets that immediately capture recruiter attention.

Improve my professional summary, work experience, measurable achievements,
project descriptions, keywords, technical skills, formatting, and business
impact. Explain how every improvement increases interview opportunities while
remaining completely truthful.
```

**Rule:** Every bullet should follow *action → scope → metric → business outcome*. If Claude adds a metric you cannot defend in an interview, delete it.

## 4. LinkedIn authority builder

Recruiters search before they reply. This prompt targets discoverability without cringe posting.

```text
Act as a LinkedIn personal branding strategist. Optimize my LinkedIn profile to
attract recruiters, hiring managers, and career opportunities.

Create my headline, About section, experience summaries, featured projects,
skills strategy, recommendation plan, networking approach, and content strategy.
Explain how each optimization strengthens my credibility and increases recruiter
visibility.
```

**Practical limit:** Implement headline + About + three experience rewrites first. Content strategy can wait until you are interview-active.

## 5. AI job search workflow

Treat Claude as a **drafting layer**, not an autopilot applicant.

```text
Act as an AI-powered career assistant. Design a complete workflow using AI to
streamline my job search.

Automate company research, resume customization, cover letters, recruiter
outreach, networking messages, interview preparation, skill-gap analysis,
follow-up emails, and application tracking. Recommend where AI improves
efficiency while keeping every application personalized and truthful.
```

**My stack:** Claude for research summaries and first-draft outreach; spreadsheet or Notion for pipeline; human edit on every message that carries your name.

## 6. Interview mastery

The highest ROI prompt in the set—if you simulate follow-ups, not just softball questions.

```text
Act as a senior hiring manager interviewing candidates for my dream role. Conduct
realistic mock interviews including behavioral, technical, leadership, case-study,
and culture-fit questions.

Evaluate my answers, identify weaknesses, improve my storytelling, strengthen my
communication, and teach me how to demonstrate measurable business impact using
structured response frameworks. Include difficult follow-up questions commonly
asked by top employers.
```

**Framework:** STAR for behavioral, *clarify → structure → trade-offs → metrics* for system design. Ask Claude to interrupt you mid-answer like a real panel would.

## 7. Networking and hidden opportunities

Most senior roles are surfaced before they are posted. This prompt builds a system, not a one-off DM.

```text
Act as a professional networking strategist. Build a complete system for finding
hidden job opportunities through LinkedIn, professional communities, referrals,
alumni networks, conferences, recruiters, and industry events.

Create outreach templates, follow-up sequences, relationship-building frameworks,
and weekly networking routines that generate genuine professional relationships.
```

**Cadence:** Three thoughtful messages per week beats thirty copy-paste connection requests. Ask Claude to shorten templates until they sound like you on a good day.

## 8. Thirty-day career sprint

Close the loop with execution, not more prompts.

```text
Act as a career execution coach. Create a realistic 30-day action plan for
maximizing my chances of landing my ideal role.

Break the roadmap into daily and weekly objectives covering applications,
networking, portfolio improvements, interview preparation, skill development,
recruiter outreach, and follow-ups. Include KPIs, measurable milestones, common
obstacles, and contingency plans if results are slower than expected.
```

**KPIs that matter:** Replies from humans, not applications submitted. Phone screens booked, not LinkedIn likes.

## Suggested sequence

| Week | Focus | Prompts |
|------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Positioning + assets | 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 |
| 2 | Pipeline + outreach | 5 → 7 |
| 3–4 | Interviews + sprint | 6 → 8 (re-run weekly) |

Re-run prompts 3 and 6 whenever you tailor for a specific company. Keep a "master truth" doc so customized résumés do not drift.

> **Takeaway:** Claude accelerates a job search when you give it roles, constraints, and verified facts—not when you ask it to "get you hired." Use these eight prompts as a structured coaching pack: plan first, position honestly, optimize assets, workflow the tedious work, rehearse hard interviews, network with intent, and execute a 30-day sprint with real KPIs. The model is free; your credibility is not.
